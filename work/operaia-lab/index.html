OperaIA.lab — a virtual office run by AI agents

An autonomous digital office where specialized AI agents take on demands, coordinate missions and report back — made visible as a living, explorable workplace.

Client
Own product · AI startup
Role
Founder · product design & build
Timeline
2026 · ongoing
Tools
Figma, Vue 3, TypeScript, Pixi.js, Fastify, PostgreSQL

View the code on GitHub ↗(opens in a new tab)

Outcome

9 AI specialists working in a live, explorable office

Context

AI agents can already research, write and automate — but for small teams they are invisible: nobody knows what they are doing, who asked for it, or whether it worked. OperaIA.lab is my answer: a digital organization of specialized agents with a persistent operational layer, shown as an office you can walk through.

Problem

How do you make autonomous work understandable and trustworthy for people who don’t read logs?

  • Agent state lived in queues and traces, not in any interface
  • Hand-offs between agents (and humans) were opaque
  • There was no single place to see “what needs me right now”

My role

I designed and built it end to end as founder: product vision, information architecture, the Command Center UI, the isometric office and the TypeScript front end on top of a modular-monolith backend (mission queue, workers, supervisor, operational memory).

Process

Research → mapped what a small team actually needs from agents: delegate, follow, approve. Design → defined the agent lifecycle (idle, working, waiting for a human, done, failed) and a “state of your office” home. Build → shipped the office with Pixi.js behind a ports-and-adapters layer, so the UI could be designed against mock data before the backend existed. Measure → heartbeat and health checks show whether the office is really operating — not just rendering.

Key decisions

  1. Make agent state spatial. Each specialist — CEO Opera, CTO Mag, Product Designer Luna, PM Nexus and others — has a desk. Status is readable at a glance, like walking into a room.
  2. Humans approve, agents execute. Sensitive actions land in Approvals; the home only asks for what truly needs you (“1 item needs you”).
  3. Brain first, interfaces later. The core operational layer is stabilized before new surfaces are added — a rule written into the project itself.

Outcome

  • A Command Center that answers “what is the state of my office?” in one screen
  • A digital team of 9 specialists, each with role, projects and last action
  • An isometric office with keyboard navigation and live agent presence
  • A modular backend with persistent missions, continuous supervision and memory, running in production

Learnings

In AI products, trust is the core UX problem. Visibility, reversible actions and a clear “what needs me” beat raw model capability every time.
