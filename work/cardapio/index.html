Cardápio — a storefront and back office for artisan bakeries

A mobile-first store in the style of delivery apps, plus a real-time back office for orders, menu, stock, recipe costing and pricing — built for one-person businesses.

Client
Small food business · confectionery
Role
Product design & full-stack build
Timeline
2026
Tools
Figma, Vue 3, Vuetify, TypeScript, Fastify, Prisma, PostgreSQL

View the code on GitHub ↗(opens in a new tab)

Outcome

8 of 11 modules ready — from menu to Pix checkout

Context

Small bakeries sell through WhatsApp and Instagram DMs: prices in a notebook, orders in chat, stock in the owner’s head. Delivery marketplaces solve discovery but take a big cut and own the customer relationship.

Problem

  • Orders get lost in chat, and the owner can’t see the day at a glance
  • Nobody knows the real cost of a brigadeiro — so prices are guessed and margins disappear
  • The customer experience depends on how fast the owner answers messages

My role

Product design and full-stack build: flows, UI for both sides (store and back office), data model and implementation with Vue 3 + Vuetify on the front and Fastify + Prisma + PostgreSQL on the back.

Process

Research → listed the real jobs of a one-person bakery: take orders, cook, deliver, get paid, restock. Design → a store that feels like the delivery apps customers already know, and a back office that fits on a laptop at the kitchen counter. Build → shipped module by module, with domain rules (cart, pricing, Pix, opening hours) covered by unit tests. Measure → the dashboard shows sales, average ticket, best sellers and estimated margin from the recipes.

Key decisions

  1. Familiar over novel. The store follows the delivery-app pattern — categories, photos, options, bag — so there is nothing to learn.
  2. Price from the recipe. Each recipe pulls ingredient costs from stock and suggests a price for the target margin; when an ingredient gets more expensive, the margin updates.
  3. A board for the day. Orders move across New → Preparing → Ready / On the way, with a sound alert for new ones and automatic stock deduction.

Outcome

  • 8 of 11 planned modules ready: menu, stock, recipe costing, real-time orders, dashboard, settings, store and checkout (Pix, card, cash, WhatsApp)
  • Delivery fees by neighborhood, pre-orders and pause-the-store controls
  • Next: coupons, reviews and per-km delivery fees

Learnings

For small businesses, the most valuable screen isn’t the storefront — it’s the recipe costing. Showing the real margin changed how prices are set.
