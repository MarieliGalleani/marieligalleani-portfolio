## Context

Founders of growing companies have data everywhere — ERP, CRM, ad platforms, bank — and still start the day guessing. BI tools answer questions you already know to ask. OperaIA flips that: the AI **speaks first**, every morning, with a decision.

## Problem

Dashboards create work instead of removing it.

- CEOs don't have time to explore charts to find the one problem that matters
- Data from different tools never lines up (different names, periods, currencies)
- Insights without a recommended action and its financial impact rarely get acted on

## My role

Founder: product strategy, the beta definition, UX and UI of every flow, and the front end. I wrote the product constitution and roadmap that decide what goes in — and what doesn't.

## Process

**Define →** a single beta promise: *sign up → connect a spreadsheet → get today's decision → talk to an advisor* in under five minutes the first time, under a minute after that.
**Design →** onboarding by connectors, a visible data pipeline (connectors → ingestion → normalization → validation → AI engine → agents → insights) and a daily cockpit.
**Build →** monorepo with a React front end and a NestJS + Prisma + PostgreSQL API; n8n normalizes incoming data.
**Measure →** the product tracks what matters for the promise: time to first decision and next-day return.

## Key decisions

1. **One decision, not twenty widgets.** The cockpit opens with a single *critical diagnosis*: the cause, what to do, and what's at stake in money.
2. **Show the machine working.** The "Second Brain" sync makes the AI's preparation visible — memory, integrations, indicators — so the answer feels earned, not magic.
3. **Specialized agents.** FinanceIA, MarketingIA and CustomerIA each own a domain, with their own insights and history of executed actions.

## Outcome

- A daily cockpit with diagnosis, action and impact on one screen
- 8 connectors designed for the most common Brazilian SMB stack (Bling, HubSpot, Meta Ads, Google Ads, banks, Slack, SAP)
- Analytics, customer health and a full history of what the agents did
- A clear, written beta scope that keeps the product focused

## Learnings

Writing the *definition of done* for the beta — in minutes, not features — was the most useful design artifact of the project. It made every "should we add this?" easy to answer.
